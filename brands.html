THREE WAYS TO SUPPORT

One garden can matter.
A connected landscape can do more.

Partnerships would be shaped around real sites, willing stewards and agreed ecological work. Each pathway starts with an assessment of what exists and what support can sustain or improve.

01 · GARDEN

Support ecological stewardship.

Help an established garden maintain its indigenous planting, living soil and habitat features. Fund a defined care programme and the record of its condition.

A practical place to begin with a local steward.

02 · STEPPING STONE

Strengthen a habitat link.

Support a garden with the potential to help bridge habitat patches. Planting, habitat and maintenance would be chosen for the place and the species it can support.

Location alone does not prove ecological connectivity; establishment and function need evidence.

03 · CORRIDOR

Support a connected group.

Help a cohort of gardens retain and develop habitat between existing green spaces. Agree a landscape plan, individual care responsibilities and follow-up across the group.

Corridor claims require ecological review. Funding and proximity are recorded separately from demonstrated function.

WHAT LOCAL SUPPORT CAN HELP SUSTAIN

Food. Shelter. Living ground.
The work continues between visits.

01

Plants of this place

Indigenous planting and the care needed for it to establish.

02

Habitat resources

Layered vegetation and appropriate shelter, nesting and water features.

03

Soil and water care

Work to support soil condition, infiltration and water-sensitive planting.

04

Landscape connections

Assessing and strengthening opportunities between habitat patches.

05

Long-term stewardship

Maintenance, replacement planting and repeat observations.

The ecological benefit depends on the site, the work and what survives. Species lists, condition assessments, survival observations and verified records can show progress. Cooling, water savings or fauna movement would need their own evidence before being claimed.

Context: City of Melbourne · urban forest and biodiversity. These are ecological opportunities, not quantified outcomes promised by a sponsorship.

THE VALUE FOR A BRAND

A local contribution.
A lasting, visible record.

For a national brand, the opportunity is to support ecological care in the neighbourhoods where its staff, customers and communities live.

PLACE

Support something close to home.

Choose a locality relevant to your organisation and fund specific work with participating stewards. Make the contribution concrete: a place, a purpose and a period of care.

EVIDENCE

Show what your support enabled.

An agreed reporting scope could document funded activities, baseline condition, establishment and follow-up. Keep observed results separate from future targets.

PEOPLE

Connect staff with the work.

Explore steward-led learning, field updates or suitable volunteering where participants consent. Build understanding of local plants, habitat and ongoing care.

STORY

Tell a specific, grounded story.

With steward permission, share the work and its progress through field notes and agreed images. Recognition would identify the contribution without implying ownership of the garden or its biodiversity.

CONTINUITY

Build support beyond a campaign.

A multi-season partnership can fund the less visible work: tending, replacing failed planting and returning to assess condition. The record develops as the garden does.

ACCOUNTABILITY

Keep a record your team can use.

Document funding, delivery and assessed outcomes for your internal reporting and communications. Sponsor acknowledgement and ecological verification remain distinct.

Our proposed communications approach follows the ACCC’s guidance on clear, specific and evidence-backed environmental claims. Sponsorship is a contribution to agreed work; it does not by itself establish an offset, credit or company-wide “nature positive” status.

YIELD · THE SUPPORT MODEL

Fund the care.
Follow the evidence.

Yield is being developed to connect brand funding with the stewards maintaining ecological gardens. Its purpose is to support ongoing ecological function and the people doing that work.

Agree where funding goes.

A costed partnership would separate stewardship support, defined ecological works, assessment and reporting, and programme administration. Allocation, payment arrangements and responsibilities would be agreed before funds are committed.

Return to the same record.

Start with the site's condition and care plan. Record delivery and follow-up, including setbacks. Public acknowledgement, stories and visits would be agreed with the steward; private addresses and contact details stay protected.

CURRENT STATUS

We are seeking founding sponsorship conversations. There is no active Yield payout programme, fixed return or published sponsorship price. A proposal would define the scope, budget, evidence and reporting before a partnership begins.

A PROPOSED PARTNERSHIP RECORD

Know what was funded.
Know what was found.

01

The starting condition

Garden identity, ecological context, measured inputs, retained assets, known limitations and the verification level of the evidence.

02

The agreed contribution

Funded care and work, budget allocation, responsible parties, follow-up dates and permitted sponsor acknowledgement.

03

The documented follow-up

Delivery, planting survival, condition, recorded habitat and other agreed observations. Report shortfalls as well as progress; distinguish maintenance of existing value from new improvement.

Sponsorship would not purchase points, verification or a claim to compensate for environmental harm elsewhere. Ecological assessments follow the Registry method, with conflicts and evidence limitations disclosed.
