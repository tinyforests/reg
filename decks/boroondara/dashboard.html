02 · Vegetation structure across the record

Every assessment records five layers. Boroondara's gardens are missing the middle one.

Each figure is the share of registered gardens recording vegetation in that stratum. Select a layer for what grows there, and how many gardens have none of it.

03 · What one assessment actually records

Thirty-one fields per garden, five pillars, one score.

The bar behind each field is how many registered gardens carry that observation. Where a bar runs clay, the field is recorded but the answer is a gap — an opportunity the record has already located for you.

04 · The observations underneath the score

Habitat features, weed pressure and fauna — counted across the whole municipality.

Each of these is a tick-box or a note on a single garden assessment. Aggregated, they become a work program: what to supply, what to remove, what's already returning.

Habitat features

Present on how many gardens. The short bars are the cheapest wins in the municipality.

Weed pressure

Environmental weeds named during assessment. Priority is frequency across gardens, not severity on one.

Fauna recorded

Reported by stewards or observed at assessment. Not a survey — a signal of what the plantings are pulling in.

Where the evidence came from, who is already asking, and what moves.

Source and verification are recorded separately from the score, and never aggregated as equivalent. Provisional records stay quarantined.

A provisional record counts as a garden on the register but never contributes to a verified municipal figure. The quarantine is structural, not cosmetic.
