01 · Ecological network

A place for private gardens in the wider landscape.

As residents opt in, the pilot can build a record of planting, habitat and ecological context. Proximity can identify potential connections; it does not prove wildlife movement.

Context map only

The record starts with consent.

No household locations or invented pilot gardens are plotted. A municipal record is not automatically a pilot participant.

A pilot map would require consented records and reviewed display locations. Habitat links and corridor outcomes remain unmeasured.

Proposed · 20 gardens

Visit → record → action → update.

A manageable first cohort to test whether an ongoing ecological record adds value to Gardens for Wildlife. The twenty gardens are a proposed scope; no scores, locations, uptake or improvement are projected.

01 · Invite

Council keeps its list.

Council invites residents through its own channels. Interested stewards choose to approach the Registry themselves.

02 · Record

Make a dated baseline.

Stewards provide their own assessment and evidence. Missing inputs remain unknown, and the source is distinguished from Registry verification.

03 · Return

See what changes.

Agree a follow-up interval, provisionally around 12 months, to record establishment, losses and practical improvements.

Pilot evaluation

Useful evidence before expansion.

Review participant experience, staff time, completeness of baselines, repeatability of assessments and usefulness of the council summary. Compare the same gardens over time, with evidence quality and missing follow-ups stated.
