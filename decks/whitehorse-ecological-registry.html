01 · The premise

Every garden
matters.

Thousands of private gardens already support birds, insects, reptiles, frogs and healthy soils.

Together, they can strengthen habitat between reserves, streets and neighbourhoods.

The question is how we recognise that contribution over time.

02 · Start with what exists

You are already
doing the work.

01 · Visit

Trusted people enter the garden.

Garden Guides and council officers meet residents on site.

02 · Understand

Habitat is seen in context.

Existing vegetation, habitat values and opportunities are discussed.

03 · Support

Residents are helped to act.

Advice and a garden report turn observation into practical change.

The Registry should support this work — not recreate it.

03 · The opportunity

What happens
after the visit?

Today

The visit creates knowledge that helps a resident understand and improve their garden.

OBSERVATION→ ADVICE→ REPORT→ ACTION
The Registry adds

A persistent ecological baseline that can be revisited, evidenced and carried forward.

BASELINE→ EVIDENCE→ CHANGE→ MEMORY

Not another assessment. A longer life for the information.

04 · The method

Record first.
Score second.

The Registry keeps the underlying observations, evidence source and date. A score is then derived from those inputs — not the other way around.

01Garden structure
02Soil & water
03Habitat features
04Connectivity
05Evidence provenance

Steward submission · designer record · site visit — the source stays attached.

05 · The record

More than a score

Every registered garden becomes a living record — held, updated and carried forward.

01Photographs
02Field notes
03Habitat observations
04Planting history
05Changes over time

A garden shouldn't lose its memory every time ownership changes.

11 · The bigger picture

This is how biodiversity is rebuilt in cities.

Not one enormous reserve. Thousands of small places becoming living systems.

A front garden.
A backyard.
A nature strip.
A school ground.
A development site.
A council reserve.

Each one small. Together, an urban ecological network.

12 · The standard we don't yet have

We already measure what we build.

Buildings
Measured
  • Energy efficiency
  • Water performance
  • Thermal comfort
  • Safety and access
Land
Mostly invisible
  • Canopy retained or lost
  • Habitat removed or rebuilt
  • Soil and water function
  • Contribution to the wider landscape

For a century we've measured what we build on a block. We've never measured what was underneath it.

13 · The future question

14 · From cleared block to living system

A record that follows the land.

01Before developmentCanopy, vegetation, habitat and soil condition — recorded before the machines arrive.
02During designAn ecological target for what the property should give back.
03After constructionThe garden is established and evidence is recorded.
04Three years laterThe living system is measured again as it grows.
05Property transferThe ecological record travels with the garden.

The score is not the important part. The record is.

15 · The long-term ambition

A common language for private-land ecology.

HomeownersUnderstand what their garden contributes.
Designers & developersSet a measurable ecological target.
CouncilsSee private-land contribution at municipal scale.
Future ownersInherit the garden's ecological memory.

One methodology, published openly and free to apply. One evidence standard. A record that survives programs, projects and ownership.

Thank you

The vision is simple.

Every garden recognised for the life that it supports.

A shared ecological record for private land — so we know not only what was built, but what the land is giving back.
