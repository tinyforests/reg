Acknowledgement of Country

At Gardener & Son, we acknowledge the Wurundjeri Woi Wurrung people of the Kulin Nation, the Traditional Custodians of the land on which we live and work.

We honour their enduring connection to Country — and the grasslands, woodlands, and wetlands that once flourished here through generations of care and balance.

These living systems were not lost to time, but to disconnection.

Our work seeks to rekindle that relationship, restoring native ecologies and weaving biodiversity back into the urban fabric — each garden a quiet act of remembrance and renewal.

01 · The premise

Every garden
matters.

Thousands of private gardens already support birds, insects, reptiles, frogs and healthy soils.

Together they form one of Australia's largest ecological networks.

Yet almost none of them are recognised.

04 · The method

Making the invisible visible

The Registry translates evidence about a garden into a consistent ecological record — held across five areas.

P1

Biodiversity structure

Indigenous species count, layers, and provenance of planting.

P2

Soil & water

Soil health, mulch, permeability, water capture and retention.

P3

Habitat complexity

Logs, rocks, hollows, ponds, leaf litter, nesting resources.

P4

Ecological connectivity

Distance to reserves, corridors and neighbouring habitat.

P5

Supporting evidence

Photographs, field notes, observations — scored separately.

Because what we measure, we value.

Evidence source recorded · Verification status retained
