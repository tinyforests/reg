Yarra Ranges · the private-land picture. 61,570 private dwellings across a 2,470 km² shire — most of the shire’s habitat sits on land council can’t reach through public programs alone, and its ecological contribution is largely unrecorded. The Registry gives each garden a five-pillar record — biodiversity structure, soil & water, habitat complexity, connectivity and evidence — and keeps it over time. Gardens for Wildlife already gathers most of this; the register is what gives it a second life. The map and figures below are an illustrative model of that record — there are no gardens on the Yarra Ranges register yet. Context: ABS 2021 Census; Yarra Ranges Gardens for Wildlife.

Selected record

Select any garden on the map to open its record — score, pillar breakdown, evidence source and who assessed it.

Where the evidence comes from

Source and verification status are recorded separately from the score. A council assessment is third-party evidence; it is not the same as a Registry verification, and the dashboard never shows the two as equivalent.

Weed pressure recorded on private land

Environmental weeds noted during garden assessments. Council currently sees these one report at a time; here they aggregate into a removal priority across the municipality.

What the gardens are actually doing

Mean score across five pillars, 0–100. The brass tick is the Yarra Ranges baseline — the average suburban block before any ecological work.

Connectivity is the pillar a single garden cannot move on its own. It only rises when neighbours plant too — which is exactly what a municipal picture reveals and a single visit report cannot.

Residents are already asking

Address lookups from Yarra Ranges postcodes across findmyevc.com and findmyecologicalgarden.com — people finding out which vegetation community their block sits in.

Change over time

The point of a register is the second visit. Each line is one garden's score across successive assessments — the ecological equivalent of a meter reading.

Yarra Ranges's Gardens for Wildlife visit already produces most of this information. The register is what gives it a second life — and a third, and a tenth.
