WHY A RECORD

A garden does not peak on the day it is photographed.

Design practices are documented at their most photogenic moment and rarely afterwards. What a naturalistic garden does over the following decade — species establishing, canopy closing, habitat arriving — is the part no portfolio has been able to show.

CORRIDOR CONTRIBUTION

Value that crosses the boundary fence.

Gardens are assessed individually, but connectivity is scored relationally. Where a practice has several gardens within reach of each other, the corridor value exceeds the sum of the parts — and that emergent value belongs to whoever created it. A public corridor map already runs on the Registry; the practice view reads your own gardens against it.

UPGRADE ROUTING

The gardens keep sending work back.

A Registry profile surfaces what a garden could do next. When a steward decides to act, the enquiry goes first to the practice that made the garden. Attribution stops being credit and becomes a standing relationship with every garden you have built.

01 · OPPORTUNITY

The record shows a gap

A garden’s profile surfaces what it could do next — a missing layer, an absent habitat feature, a water function it lacks.

02 · STEWARD DECIDES

Entirely their call

The steward chooses whether to act at all. Nothing routes anywhere without them raising the enquiry.

03 · FIRST REFUSAL

Routed to your practice

The original designer has fourteen days to respond. Contact details appear once the enquiry is accepted; released enquiries pass to Gardener & Son.

04 · RECORD UPDATES

Reassessed, not claimed

Completed work is confirmed and reassessed. The score moves because the garden changed, never because a claim was made.

The routing queue is part of the pilot portal. No commercial terms attach to taking part, and Yield payments are not active.

THREE ACTORS, ONE RECORD

How attribution works.

Every garden records three roles separately, and none can overwrite another. That separation is what makes the record citable — and what makes a practice’s attribution worth holding.

THE DESIGNER

Permanent attribution

The practice that made the garden. Attribution is canonical and survives sale of the property — a garden changes hands, the record of who designed it does not.

THE STEWARD

Consent and control

The household holding the garden. They control permission, what appears publicly, and whether a submission proceeds at all.

THE VERIFIER

Named independence

Whoever assessed it, recorded explicitly. Review means a submission has been read; verification is a separate step requiring evidence and an explicit reviewer.

Stewards stay in control.

The garden’s street address, the steward’s email, evidence links and reviewer notes are all held privately. A public profile shows the garden’s agreed name, suburb and ecological record. A submission requires the steward’s permission and their consent to a public profile, confirmed at the point of submitting. Provisional and design-proposal records are unverified, shown as such, and excluded from every verified total. Practice access can be revoked or replaced by the Registry at any time.

01 · INVITATION

Open your practice portal.

Gardener & Son provides a private access link, or you can request a one-use link to your practice’s contact email. The portal shows your practice’s records and nobody else’s.

02 · SUBMISSION

Document what is there.

With steward permission, add known measurements, a species list and an evidence link. Unassessed inputs can remain unknown — they simply contribute no points. No new site visit is needed to begin.

03 · REVIEW

Build a public record.

Submissions stay private during review. A published garden receives a profile; verification requires supporting evidence and an explicit reviewer.
