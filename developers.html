THE MISSING CONTINUITY

A plan is a beginning.
The garden is the outcome.

Garden-area, canopy and vegetation controls already exist. The next step is to connect them to a dated record of the site before development and the living landscape after it.

A drawing cannot establish plant survival, recovering soil or functioning habitat. The proposed pilot would check delivery and establishment, with responsibility for follow-up agreed from the outset.

Existing Victorian context: garden-area requirements and canopy-tree protections. Applicable requirements depend on the site and proposal.

PROPOSED GOVERNING PRINCIPLES · VERSION 0.1

Keep what matters.
Make the promise testable.

These draft principles will be tested through a pilot and refined with independent input.

01 · THE BASELINE

Record before disturbance.

Document the site before clearance, demolition or earthworks. An incomplete or retrospective baseline is labelled honestly.

02 · RETENTION

Protect existing value first.

Keep mature trees, valuable habitat and soil in view. New planting and extra points cannot automatically compensate for their loss.

03 · PLACE

Measure against the place.

Use the local ecological community and site constraints. A grassland and a woodland need different reference conditions.

04 · EVIDENCE

Separate forecast from fact.

A design target, an installed landscape and established ecological function are distinct claims, each with a date and evidence.

05 · VERIFICATION

Make assessment independent.

Record developer, designer, steward and verifier separately. Test a method that trained assessors can apply consistently.

06 · RESPONSIBILITY

Plan beyond handover.

Agree who maintains planting, replaces failures, funds reassessment and remedies shortfalls through establishment.

07 · LANDSCAPE

Show contribution and loss.

Report area, retained assets and all five pillars. A higher score alone is not proof of biodiversity net gain or a functioning corridor.

08 · CONTINUITY

Carry the record forward.

Give the next steward the ecological history and care plan. Publish only agreed information; keep addresses and contact details private.

FROM SITE TO STEWARDSHIP

Four checks.
One ecological history.

01

Before works

Record existing species, layers, canopy, soil and water observations, habitat features and surrounding patches. Identify retained assets and uncertainties.

BASELINE · EXISTING CONDITION
02

During design

Set explicit protection measures, planting and habitat specifications, evidence requirements and establishment targets. Keep future performance clearly labelled.

PROPOSAL · NOT A VERIFIED OUTCOME
03

At installation and handover

Inspect what is installed, record departures from the plan and name the responsible caretaker. Young planting is recorded as young planting.

AS BUILT · DELIVERY CHECK
04

After establishment

Return at agreed intervals to document survival, actual canopy, habitat and condition. Suggested pilot checks are around 12 and 24 months after planting, with longer monitoring where needed.

ENERGY RATINGS

Research became a usable standard.

Five-star requirements entered the national Building Code in 2006, supported by regulatory analysis. The scheme continued to be evaluated and strengthened. Victoria implemented newer seven-star provisions in May 2024.

OUR PROPOSED PATH

Start with a measured pilot.

Bring developers, council officers, independent ecologists, research partners and Traditional Owners into method development. Test consistency, outcomes, cost and practical responsibility before seeking policy recognition.

Future planning recognition or disclosure would require the relevant government process. Participation, funding and assessment arrangements will be agreed before a pilot begins.

  1. Publish and review the method
  2. Test it on consenting sites
  3. Evaluate delivery and establishment
  4. Consider government recognition
