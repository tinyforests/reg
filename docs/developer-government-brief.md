# A lasting ecological record for development sites

**Government discussion brief · 12 Oct 2026 · Ecological Registry / Gardener & Son**
**Proposal for a Victorian pilot; no government endorsement or statutory status is claimed.**

## The request

Convene a scoping meeting with a willing council, Victorian planning and environment officers, an independent ecological research partner and development representatives. Develop a costed pilot that records site ecology before works, checks delivery, and returns after establishment. Agree the governance and evaluation method before enrolling sites.

The initial ask is evidence-building infrastructure, not immediate adoption of a private rating as a legal development requirement. If the pilot proves useful and fair, its published method can inform future procurement, planning policy and disclosure. Government must determine the appropriate legal mechanism.

## The proposition

Buildings have a recognised energy-performance framework. The living ground around them deserves a documented performance history too.

Existing controls already address parts of this problem: garden area, canopy, vegetation protection and environmentally sustainable design. The Registry proposes a complementary record connecting the **before**, the **proposed**, the **delivered** and the **established** landscape. Victoria's current canopy protections are an important starting point, not a reason to claim the field is unregulated. [Victorian canopy guidance](https://www.planning.vic.gov.au/guides-and-resources/guides/all-guides/protecting-and-enhancing-our-tree-canopy-for-a-greener-victoria).

A landscape plan expresses intent. A continuing, independently checked record can show whether that intent was delivered and sustained. That is the practical gap to test.

## Proposed governing principles

1. **Record before disturbance.** Establish a dated, independently reviewed baseline before demolition, clearance or earthworks. An already-cleared site must be marked as having an incomplete baseline; it cannot be treated as evidence that nothing was lost.
2. **Retain valuable existing ecology first.** Record mature trees, remnant vegetation, soil and habitat features individually. A higher aggregate score cannot compensate automatically for their loss or authorise removal.
3. **Assess against the place.** Use the site's pre-clearing ecological community, climate, area and constraints. A grassland should not be forced to resemble woodland merely to collect canopy points.
4. **Separate intent from outcome.** Keep proposed performance, verified installation and established function in distinct dated assessments. Predicted mature canopy is not existing canopy. Future connectivity is not confirmed movement of fauna.
5. **Verify independently.** Identify the developer, designer, steward and verifier separately. Establish assessor competence, conflict rules, evidence requirements, audit sampling and an appeal route. The scheme author should not be the sole authority deciding whether its clients passed.
6. **Name responsibility through establishment.** Specify who maintains planting, replaces failures, funds reassessment and remedies a shortfall; record handover to the future steward or owners corporation. These are proposed pilot commitments, with any enforceability separately documented.
7. **Measure contribution without hiding loss.** Report the five pillars alongside absolute area and retained assets. A small high-scoring garden cannot by itself demonstrate that a much larger habitat loss was repaired. Improvement in points is not proof of biodiversity net gain.
8. **Make the record portable and consent-based.** Maintain the ecological history across handover, without transferring personal details automatically. Public information is consented and locality-based; exact addresses and contact details stay private.

## What would be measured

| Assessment stage | Evidence | Decision supported |
|---|---|---|
| Before works | Boundaries, retained assets, indigenous flora, structural layers, soil/water observations, existing habitat and surrounding patches | Establish what exists and what must be retained or investigated |
| Design proposal | Landscape specification, protection measures, area allocations, planting community, future maintenance and dated targets | Compare the proposal with baseline and existing planning requirements |
| Installation / handover | Site inspection, installed species and features, survival at inspection, as-built changes and named caretaker | Identify delivery shortfalls; do not claim mature ecological function |
| Establishment | Repeat observations at agreed intervals, survival/replacement, actual canopy and habitat, condition, maintenance and documented connectivity | Test whether delivery persists and agree remediation |

The Registry's existing five pillars are biodiversity structure, soil and water, habitat complexity, connectivity, and evidence and verification. The current 0–100 score is an existing method for garden records. It is **not** a validated statutory biodiversity metric, a replacement for native-vegetation regulation, an offset unit or a government energy-rating equivalent.

Before a compliance threshold could be proposed, research must test reference-relative benchmarks, agreement between assessors, sensitivity to site size, missing data and the ecological relevance of the score. Separate hard protections and pillar floors would need evaluation; favourable evidence points must not conceal poor ecological condition.

## Pilot design to scope together

**Proposed starting cohort:** 15–25 consenting development sites across one or two willing municipalities, with more than one developer and a range of site sizes and baseline conditions. These are suggested scoping figures, not confirmed participation or a statistically powered trial. An independent evaluator must determine the sample and comparison design.

**Duration:** allow a baseline/design phase, checks at installation and proposed follow-ups around 12 and 24 months after planting. Construction schedules and ecology may require longer observation. A twelve-month snapshot cannot establish mature canopy or long-term habitat recovery.

**Comparison:** where feasible, use matched non-pilot developments and repeated measurements in comparable seasons; document differences in irrigation, maintenance, drought and development type. Evaluate both individual pillars and raw measures. Publish attrition and failed planting, not only successful examples.

**Evaluation outcomes:** retained mature assets; indigenous species confirmed as appropriate to place; establishment survival; actual vegetated and permeable area; measured canopy rather than mature projections; defensible soil/water observations; habitat persistence; assessor agreement; completion and remediation rates; assessment and maintenance costs; participant burden; and privacy performance. Fauna use, water retention and cooling need their own measurement protocols before impact claims can be made.

**Budget:** cost the protocol, independent assessments, data stewardship, follow-up inspections, research, legal/probity review and reporting. Obtain quotes and distinguish Registry fees from independent-verifier fees. No funding amount or benefit-cost ratio is proposed without this work.

## Governance and public value

Publish a versioned method, data dictionary, evidence categories and change log. Give government interoperable exports and the ability to use another provider applying the method. Develop ecological oversight with independent expertise and appropriately remunerated Traditional Owner participation. Record conflicts and separate method governance, commercial design services and verification.

The Registry is operated by Gardener & Son, which also provides garden design services. Existing council partnership commitments remain in force: councils retain participant lists; residents supply consent directly; de-identified council statistics remain separate from household records; and council-pathway participants are not marketed design services by G&S. A council evidence source is not a council endorsement or verification of a Registry score.

A pilot should test whether a persistent record improves delivery and public reporting at an acceptable cost. It should not assume future property premiums, permit shortcuts, biodiversity credits, rebates or Yield payments.

## A staged policy pathway

1. **Scope and validate:** convene partners; publish a draft protocol; obtain independent scientific, privacy, probity and planning-law review.
2. **Voluntary demonstration:** test agreed developer commitments and, where appropriate, public procurement specifications. Assess consistency and burden.
3. **Evaluate and consult:** publish methods, failures, costs and outcomes; compare with existing tools and invite industry and community scrutiny.
4. **Consider a planning instrument:** only if evidence supports it, work with planning authorities on an appropriate policy or amendment, consultation and legal drafting. An additional permit condition is not automatically lawful simply because the Registry recommends it. [Victorian amendment process](https://www.planning.vic.gov.au/planning-schemes/amendments/amending-a-planning-scheme).
5. **Consider broader recognition and disclosure:** assess state-level recognition and property-transfer disclosure separately, with consent, consumer protection and data continuity resolved. National building-code adoption would be a later question, not the pilot's assumed destination.

## The meeting's four decisions

Agree whether the gap is useful to test; identify a prospective pilot sponsor and independent evaluator; approve a scope for the protocol and costed proposal; and nominate the process for privacy, conflict-of-interest and legal review.

**Discussion position:** record what was there, retain what matters, verify what is delivered, and return to see whether it is functioning.
