# From house energy ratings to garden performance

**Research and implementation strategy · 12 Oct 2026**

## Recommendation

Follow the institutional process that made energy ratings useful: research, a repeatable method, assessment infrastructure, regulatory analysis, adoption and continuing evaluation. Keep the existing garden rating scale while testing whether it can support a future standard. Do not relabel a 0–100 score as an energy-style five-star certificate or call it a government-approved ecological measure.

The proposed developer pathway is **baseline → design commitment → installed outcome → establishment checks → continuing handover record**. The immediate public page invites pilot discussions; it does not offer automatic development certification.

## What happened with building energy ratings

| Milestone | What the primary sources establish | Practical lesson for the Registry |
|---|---|---|
| 1993 / 2004 | CSIRO records NatHERS beginning in 1993 and becoming the nationwide approach in 2004. [CSIRO history](https://www.csiro.au/en/news/all/articles/2021/september/its-in-the-stars-how-scientists-figure-out-your-homes-energy-rating) | A credible method and institutions precede broad adoption. |
| 2003 | The ABCB records the introduction of minimum residential energy requirements for houses. [ABCB 2022 regulatory analysis](https://www.abcb.gov.au/sites/default/files/resources/2022/Energy%20Efficiency%202022%20Decision%20RIS%20Publication%20version%20%28359067.2%29.pdf) | Standards require a regulatory vehicle, not just public enthusiasm. |
| 2004–05 in Victoria | Victoria's evaluation describes the phase-in of five-star thermal and associated water/energy measures between July 2004 and July 2005. [Victorian six-star evaluation](https://www.energy.vic.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0017/592010/Evaluation-of-Victorian-6-Star-Standard-Final-Report.pdf) | State implementation and transition periods matter. |
| 2006 nationally | The Commonwealth identifies a five-star requirement in the Building Code of Australia, supported by a Regulation Impact Statement. [DCCEEW five-star evaluation summary](https://www.dcceew.gov.au/energy/publications/evaluation-5-star-energy-efficiency-standard-residential-buildings) | Test costs, benefits and alternatives before mandating a threshold. |
| 2010 | The ABCB records the national move to six-star residential provisions; jurisdictional implementation differs. [ABCB analysis](https://www.abcb.gov.au/sites/default/files/resources/2022/Energy%20Efficiency%202022%20Decision%20RIS%20Publication%20version%20%28359067.2%29.pdf) | Raise expectations in stages, using evidence and industry readiness. |
| NCC 2022 / Victoria 2024 | Building Ministers agreed the newer provisions in 2022. Victoria implemented seven-star and whole-of-home requirements on 1 May 2024. [ABCB decision](https://www.abcb.gov.au/ncc-2022-residential-energy-efficiency-final-decision-ris), [Victorian implementation](https://www.energy.vic.gov.au/households/save-with-all-electric-home/electric-and-efficiency-standards-for-buildings/7-star-energy-efficiency-building-standards) | Coordinate national technical rules with the authority responsible for local implementation. |

This is not a simple story in which voluntary disclosure alone produced the minimum standard. Minimum requirements were incorporated into building regulation. Disclosure is a distinct, complementary policy stream. The Commonwealth has published a separate home-energy disclosure framework. [Disclosure framework](https://www.energy.gov.au/sites/default/files/2024-12/home-energy-ratings-disclosure-framework-version-2.pdf).

## What made the model adoptable

**Consistent measurement.** NatHERS has benchmark software, protocols and climate-specific rating bands. Its 2022 starband work tested designs across 69 climate zones. This supports the inference that garden benchmarks should be calibrated to ecological context rather than assume that the same species count means the same outcome everywhere. [NatHERS starbands](https://www.homeenergyrating.gov.au/resources/nathers-2022-starbands).

**An assessment profession.** The official assessor-accrediting protocol supports assessments for regulatory use. Garden assessors will likewise need documented competence, field procedures, evidence rules, quality assurance and correction mechanisms. This is an analogy, not an existing Registry accreditation scheme. [Current assessor protocol](https://www.homeenergyrating.gov.au/resources/AAO-protocol).

**An economic and institutional case.** Regulatory analysis considers options against a status quo, rather than assuming a chosen threshold is worthwhile. The developer pilot should measure cost per assessment, follow-up and remediation; administrative burden; and credible environmental outcomes. A garden business case cannot simply borrow energy-bill savings or an energy policy's benefit-cost ratio. [ABCB final decision RIS](https://www.abcb.gov.au/ncc-2022-residential-energy-efficiency-final-decision-ris).

**Evaluation after adoption.** CSIRO's 2013 study assessed 414 houses, with energy monitoring in 209. It found winter benefits but mixed summer results and called for further research. The distinction between modelled performance and occupied outcomes mattered. For gardens, installation compliance cannot establish plant survival, mature habitat or persistent ecological benefit. [CSIRO five-star report, executive summary](https://www.dcceew.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/evaluation-5-star-energy-efficiency-standard-residential-buildings-report.pdf).

**Data that accumulates.** CSIRO's housing portal collected rating certificates from 2016 and passed one million in 2021. A comparable garden ledger should preserve method versions, dated assessments, evidence and changes rather than overwrite the past. [CSIRO certificate dataset](https://www.csiro.au/en/news/All/Articles/2021/December/one-million-energy-rating-certificates-on-australian-housing-data-portal).

“Success” here means an adopted, used and progressively evaluated system. It does not mean every predicted benefit was realised, that rating alone caused every improvement, or that energy and biodiversity can be measured interchangeably.

## Existing garden and landscape requirements

There is existing accountability, with different coverage and different measures. Victoria has a minimum garden-area requirement in relevant zones. Garden area can include features such as pools and paving, so it is not itself a complete ecological-condition measure. [Minimum garden-area guidance](https://www.planning.vic.gov.au/guides-and-resources/guides/planning-practice-notes/applying-the-minimum-garden-area-requirement).

The residential codes include canopy provisions, and clause 52.37 was introduced in September 2025 for canopy-tree protection in specified circumstances. Existing rules and exemptions must be assessed site by site. [Canopy guidance](https://www.planning.vic.gov.au/guides-and-resources/guides/all-guides/protecting-and-enhancing-our-tree-canopy-for-a-greener-victoria), [single-home code](https://www.planning.vic.gov.au/guides-and-resources/guides/all-guides/residential-development/single-home-code).

BESS supports environmental design assessment in Victorian planning, and Melbourne's Green Factor tool assesses greening proposals. C376 material describes proposed requirements; the consulted material does not establish current final ministerial approval. We should not cite that amendment as universally operative law. [BESS](https://www.bess.net.au/about/planning-system/), [Melbourne Green Factor proposal](https://participate.melbourne.vic.gov.au/amendment-c376/green-factor-tool).

Native-vegetation controls also have an avoid–minimise–offset framework. A garden rating must not substitute for these protections or imply new planting permits otherwise prohibited clearing. [Official native-vegetation guidelines](https://planning-schemes.app.planning.vic.gov.au/static/incorporateddocs/legacy/VPP/402991/VC138-Guidelines-for-the-removal%2C-destruction-or-lopping-of-native-vegetation%2C-Dept-of-Environment%2C-Land-Water-and-Planning%2C-Dec-2017.pdf).

The defensible claim is therefore: **the Registry seeks to add site-level ecological continuity, evidence and establishment follow-up to existing controls.** It is not defensible to assert that no landscape regulation or rating tool exists, or that this is demonstrably Australia's first garden standard.

## Define “positive” before selling it

A score describes selected attributes under a method. A developer's ecological gain is a different claim. Before any “ecologically positive development” designation, test a framework combining:

- A minimum ecological condition calibrated to site type and reference community.
- A comparison with the genuine pre-works baseline, with uncertainty and incomplete baselines visible.
- Retention protections for mature and irreplaceable features that cannot be traded for points elsewhere.
- Area and extent measures so a small intense planting cannot hide a large loss.
- Individual pillar results and potential floors, preventing one strong category from masking another's failure.
- Time: temporary loss, establishment failure and maturity forecasts separately reported.
- Evidence of local network contribution where claimed; distance to a park alone does not prove functional connectivity.

None of these becomes a new formula through this page launch. Thresholds, hard gates and reference calibration are proposed research work. The current method's verification points must also be examined: confidence in evidence and ecological condition should remain distinguishable for policy use.

Protection comes first; recovery is documented. A cleared site's low starting score must not let a developer advertise recovery as if the original habitat had never existed. Late entrants can still establish a record, but retrospective evidence is labelled and cannot recreate an unmeasured past.

## Delivery and policy sequence

| Workstream | First deliverable | Gate before progressing |
|---|---|---|
| Public developer entry | Honest developer principles, lifecycle and pilot enquiry | Clear distinction between active Registry and proposed standard |
| Independent governance | Ecological, planning, research, development and Traditional Owner input; conflicts register and appeal design | Partners accept scope and independence arrangements |
| Field protocol | Reference context, measurements, dates, missing-data rules, retention register, as-built and establishment checks | Two assessors can apply it consistently; reproducibility is measured |
| Pilot | Costed cohort, consent, assessment schedule, comparison design, named maintenance responsibility | Recruitment and funding agreed; no invented commitments |
| Evaluation | Public results, failures, cost and burden, independent review | Outcomes support an appropriate policy instrument |
| Planning recognition | Options paper and lawful drafting pathway | Planning authority and government process, consultation and impact analysis |
| Broader disclosure | Property-transfer record design and personal-data boundaries | Policy authority, consumer clarity, interoperability and privacy resolved |

A voluntary developer undertaking can document responsibility without pretending to be a statutory permit condition. A council may be a pilot partner without endorsing a score. Neither a website nor a private contract binds future stewards automatically or guarantees permission to develop.

Victoria's planning-scheme amendment process provides a route to test later policy proposals, involving planning authorities, authorisation, consultation and approval. Planning-led adoption is my recommended first policy route because this proposal concerns site ecology and development landscapes; immediate insertion into the NCC is a later, unproven option. This recommendation is an inference, not government advice or a promised outcome. [Planning amendment process](https://www.planning.vic.gov.au/planning-schemes/amendments/amending-a-planning-scheme).

## First approaches and what to ask

**Council planning and urban ecology teams:** Is there a delivery/establishment evidence gap worth testing? Would a small cohort complement existing tools? What privacy and probity process is required?

**Victorian planning and environment policy teams:** Which existing controls and initiatives should the method complement? What evidence and governance would be required before any policy recognition? Seek a technical scoping discussion, not endorsement at first contact.

**Independent research and ecological practitioners:** Can they co-design reference benchmarks, reliability testing and longitudinal evaluation? Obtain permission before naming any institution as a partner.

**Developers and landscape architects:** Can a baseline be captured before disturbance? Who controls installation and maintenance, and who pays for failures and follow-up? Do not promise faster permits or asset-price gains.

**Traditional Owners:** Invite participation early through an agreed, remunerated process. A pre-clearing map is technical context; it is not cultural authority or permission to speak on behalf of Country.

No outreach has been sent and no organisation is represented as participating.
