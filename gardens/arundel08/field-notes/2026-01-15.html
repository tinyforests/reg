Ecological baseline and preparation -- return visit

Return visit months after initial clearance works. The site reveals its next chapter -- a second wave of weed growth across the bare soil, a predictable response to disturbance. Today's visit focused on establishing a clear ecological baseline: understanding the true condition of the soil, identifying which plants are present and how they are influencing the site, reducing pressure from species that would prevent recovery, observing early signs of soil life and insect activity, and recording all findings so future decisions are based on evidence rather than assumption.

Rear garden overview -- second wave growth

Months after clearance, the bare soil has responded predictably. A new flush of weeds has colonised -- this is not failure, it is succession. The goddess statue watches from the left, fountain base at centre, fence and reserve canopy beyond. The nasturtium orange visible behind shows the existing garden character persisting at the edges.

Soil sampling -- 6 samples across site zones

Six soil samples collected from different areas to capture variation in condition. The soil is currently in poor condition -- sandy, low in organic matter, limited natural structure and very limited leaf litter. As a result it holds low moisture and nutrients, reflecting a history of disturbance and clearing. Importantly, however, the soil is not dead.

Weed species identified and removed

All removals done by hand, without machinery or chemicals, to avoid further soil damage and to protect existing soil life. The species present are non-native or non-local, highly competitive in disturbed low-quality soils, and fast-seeding plants that quickly dominate open ground.

Lower garden -- nasturtiums in flower, second wave grass growth

The lower flat zone shows grass and nasturtium flowering vigorously into the bare area. Nasturtiums are edible and pollinator-friendly but competitive -- they will be managed rather than fully removed. Black water tank visible right. This is the zone closest to the brick outbuilding and shows the most vigorous regrowth.

In summary -- an honest starting point

The soil is currently poor but biologically alive. Competing plants have begun to be removed to reduce pressure. Early soil life and insects are present. The garden is now in careful, staged regeneration.

This is exactly how long-lived, healthy gardens begin -- with understanding, restraint, and a clear plan forward. The soil samples will inform amendment decisions. The weed map informs future preparation strategy.
