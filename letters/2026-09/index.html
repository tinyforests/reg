Planting
What to plant this month

September into October is the planting window in Melbourne: soil still holding winter moisture, days warming, and roots have months to settle before summer bites. Plant grasses and groundcovers first — they knit the ground and shelter everything that comes after.

On the record
A garden on the record

On Sir Garnet Road in Surrey Hills, Richard and Jenny’s front garden carries a small sign on its fence. Behind it is a narrow buffer of species that belong to that ground, and a public record of what’s growing there — scored on biodiversity, habitat, soil and water, connection, and evidence.

The sign does something the planting alone can’t: neighbours stop, read it, and ask. Several of the gardens now on the Registry started as exactly that conversation.

Enrol
Put your garden on the record

The Ecological Registry is a public record of gardens doing real ecological work. You enrol your own garden through a short online form — a few questions about what you’ve planted, your address, and a photo. Ten minutes, give or take. You don’t need a finished garden: a few plants that belong and the intention to add more is enough. The record grows with it.

The first ten gardens to enrol before 30 September get a Registry sign for their front fence, free.
