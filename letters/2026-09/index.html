Coast Banksia Woodland.
EVC 2  ·  Vulnerable  ·  Featured ecology
Behind the dunes, Rosebud. Photo: Gardener & Son.

Before the bay’s edge was cleared and built on, this is what stood behind the dunes at Rosebud, on Bunurong Country. Coast Banksia over Coast Tea-tree, Boobialla and Drooping Sheoak, with succulent creepers across the sand that help shield the banksias from fire. Left long enough without fire, coastal tea-tree scrub grows into it.

It was once widespread around the Peninsula. Now it’s scattered and rare. If your plant list came back with EVC 2, this is what your block is part of.
Where to see it
01 Rosebud and Capel Sound foreshore. Remnant patches behind the dunes along the bay, like the one above. Stay on the tracks: the dunes are fragile.
02 Bushrangers Bay track, Cape Schanck. From the Boneo Road car park, 2.5 km along Main Creek through banksia groves to the bay. Mornington Peninsula National Park.
Plants that carry meaning
Coast Banksia Banksia integrifolia
Nectar-heavy flower cones, soaked in water for a sweet drink. Coast Beard-heath Leucopogon parviflorus
Small white fruit, eaten fresh in summer. Pigface Carpobrotus rossii
Salty leaves and a fig-like fruit, both eaten.

Spring is when the ground says yes.

Soil is still holding winter moisture, days are warming, and roots have months to settle before summer bites. Plant grasses and groundcovers first. They knit the ground and shelter everything that comes after. Whatever goes in this month goes on your record.

Put your garden on the record.

A short form: what you’ve planted, where, and a photo. It doesn’t need to be finished. A few plants that belong and the intention to add more is enough. Here’s what you get:
01 A public record of your garden, scored across five pillars: biodiversity, soil and water, habitat, connectivity and evidence. Yours to share.
02 A record that grows with the garden. Add species and field notes as you plant, and watch the score move.
03 Five indigenous plants, free, for the first ten gardens to enrol. Tubestock chosen for your EVC. Collect them from our Auburn Road studio in Hawthorn, or we’ll post them anywhere in Victoria for $15.
04 First in line for Yield when it opens. More below.

■  New  ·  Yield
What if your garden was supported for the work it does for nature?

A garden full of the plants that belong where it grows does real work: habitat for birds and insects the suburbs have lost, soil that holds water, a stepping stone between parks. Nobody pays for any of it.

Yield is how we change that. We’re matching sponsors with a founding set of gardens on the Registry, so the ecological work a garden does can be supported directly: back to its steward and back into the garden.

Yield opens to gardens on the record first. The way in is to enrol.
