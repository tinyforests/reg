What it measures

Six sensors. One garden.

Each Signal pairs the sign at the gate with a small probe bed and gauge placed in the garden.

01

Soil moisture

How much water the soil is holding, and how fast it dries after rain. In a mulched, unirrigated garden, this is the number that shows the mulch working.

How it works

From the record to the garden. And back.

The Registry feeds the sign

The garden’s identity, assessed score and verification come from its Registry record. Gardener & Son install the sign and garden sensors.

It reads the garden

Every five minutes, around the clock. The intended design identifies birds on the sign itself and keeps species detections, including time and confidence, rather than audio recordings.

The sign feeds the record

Garden observations return as dated evidence on the profile card and in seasonal updates. Any score change is a separate reassessment, then shared back to the sign.

What you'll see

Your garden's own season, in numbers.

After rain

How many days each fall keeps the root zone moist, and how that changes as mulch and leaf litter build up year on year.

e.g. 18 mm on Tuesday · soil above 25% for 9 days

Through the seasons

Soil warming in spring, the first frost night, the hottest stretch of summer, all measured where the plants are growing.

e.g. soil at 15 cm passed 15°C on 24 October

Who's around

The species heard each day and over a season, when migrants arrive, and how the chorus changes as the garden matures.

e.g. 14 species this week · first Sacred Kingfisher 2 Nov

Built honest

Measured, recorded, never inflated.

  • The big number is the garden's Registry score. It changes only when the record is reassessed, never automatically from a sensor.
  • Sensor data is added to the record as evidence, labelled as measured at the garden.
  • The sign shows the record and a few live readings. The full data stays with the steward, and the garden's precise location is never published.
  • No camera. The intended audio design processes birdsong on the sign and retains species detections rather than audio recordings. This remains subject to prototype validation.
  • A dark face lights only the numerals and selected indicators, with dimming after dark. The design aims to reduce display power; battery life and solar charging remain to be tested.

The build

One sign, one probe bed, one gauge.

Display
Dark face with illuminated green numerals and selected indicators; intended to be readable from the footpath and dimmed after dark
Housing
Powder-coated aluminium, opal acrylic face, sealed against weather

Raspberry Pi, running the sensors and bird identification on board
Connection
The garden's Wi-Fi, syncing readings to the Registry
Power
Low-voltage supply for initial testing. Battery operation and solar charging are being explored; capacity and runtime are not yet established.

Founding gardens · Spring 2026

The first Signals go to Registry gardens.

We're building the first units now and placing them in a small group of registered gardens, chosen for how much their readings can tell us. What the founding gardens measure will shape the finished sign.

Pilot essentials

Who can take part
Founding units are for gardens already on the Ecological Registry. Not registered? Start with the Registry.
Location and installation
Installation is by Melbourne-based Gardener & Son. Include your suburb so we can discuss installation availability. Initial testing needs garden Wi-Fi and a low-voltage supply from the house; battery operation and solar charging are being explored; installation is expected to take half a day.

Participation costs, pilot duration, equipment ownership and maintenance arrangements are not listed here. Ask about these when enquiring, before deciding whether to take part.

Questions

Good to know.

Does my garden need to be on the Registry?

Yes. Signal shows the garden's Registry record at the gate and adds its readings to that record, so the founding units are going to registered gardens. Start with the Registry if your garden is not registered yet.

How is sound handled?

The microphone captures sound so BirdNET can identify birds on the device. The intended design retains the species name, time and confidence, rather than audio recordings, and does not upload audio. Temporary audio processing is needed for identification; the final handling of that audio is part of prototype validation.

What does the pilot cost, and who looks after the equipment?

Cost, duration, equipment ownership and maintenance terms are not published on this page. Include these questions in your enquiry so you can discuss the arrangements before deciding whether to take part.

Can the readings change our score?

Not by themselves. The score changes only when the record is reassessed. Sensor data is added as evidence, clearly labelled as measured at the garden.

What does it need from the house?

Initial testing uses a low-voltage power supply and the garden’s Wi-Fi. Battery operation and solar charging are being explored; capacity and runtime are not established. Bird identification is intended to run on the sign.

Is Signal finished?

Not yet. It's a prototype, and the founding pilot this spring is how we'll finish it. Specifications may change as the pilot runs.
