What it measures

Six sensors, all in the garden

Each Signal pairs the sign at the gate with sensors placed in the garden. Weather apps report a region; these report the garden.

Soil moisture

How much water the soil is holding, and how fast it dries after rain. In a mulched, unirrigated garden this is the number that shows the mulch working.

Sensor
Capacitive probe, no exposed metal
Placed
Root zone, 15 cm below the mulch
Reports
Volumetric water content, %
Every
5 minutes

Soil temperature

When the soil warms in spring and how much the mulch buffers summer heat and winter cold at root depth.

Sensor
Sealed stainless probe
Placed
10–15 cm, beside the moisture probe
Reports
°C, daily min / max
Every
5 minutes

Rainfall

Rain that actually fell on the garden, set against the modelled record for the area, and how long each fall stays in the soil.

Sensor
Self-emptying tipping-bucket gauge
Placed
Open ground, clear of canopy drip
Reports
mm per fall, per day, per season
Resolution
0.2 mm

Air temperature and humidity

Heat days, frost nights and dew, measured inside the garden's own microclimate under its canopy.

How much light reaches the understorey through the day and across the seasons, a direct read on how the canopy is closing over.

Birdsong

Which birds are calling, how many species a day, and when the dawn chorus starts. Calls are identified on the device; no audio is kept.

What the readings show

The garden's own season, in numbers

After rain

How many days each fall keeps the root zone moist, and how that changes as mulch and leaf litter build up year on year.

e.g. 18 mm on Tuesday · soil above 25% for 9 days

Through the seasons

Soil warming in spring, the first frost night, the hottest stretch of summer, all measured where the plants are growing.

e.g. soil at 15 cm passed 15°C on 24 October

Who's around

The species heard each day and over a season, when migrants arrive, and how the chorus changes as the garden matures.

e.g. 14 species this week · first Sacred Kingfisher 2 Nov

How it works

Measured, recorded, kept honest

  • Readings go to the garden's Ecological Registry record and appear in the steward's seasonal notes.
  • The big number on the sign is the garden's Registry score. It changes only when the record is reassessed, never automatically from a sensor.
  • Sensor data is added to the record as evidence, labelled as measured at the garden, alongside field notes and verified observations.
  • The sign shows the record and a few live readings. The full data stays with the steward's record, and the precise location of the garden is never published.
  • No camera. The microphone keeps species detections only; no sound is recorded or stored.
  • The display dims at night for neighbours and wildlife.

The build

One sign, one probe bed, one gauge

Display
Illuminated green LED panel, readable from the footpath, dimmed after dark
Housing
Powder-coated aluminium, opal acrylic face, sealed against weather
Computer
Raspberry Pi, running the sensors and bird identification on board
Connection
The garden's Wi-Fi, syncing readings to the Registry
Power
Low-voltage supply from the house
Install
Half a day by Gardener & Son: sign at the gate, probes in the root zone, gauge in the open
