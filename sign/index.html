What it measures

Six sensors. One garden.

Each Signal pairs the sign at the gate with a small probe bed and gauge placed in the garden.

01

Soil moisture

How much water the soil is holding, and how fast it dries after rain. In a mulched, unirrigated garden, this is the number that shows the mulch working.

Sensor
Capacitive probe, no exposed metal
Placed
Root zone, 15 cm below the mulch
Reports
Water content, %
02

Soil temperature

When the soil warms in spring, and how much the mulch buffers summer heat and winter cold at root depth.

Sensor
Sealed stainless probe
Placed
10–15 cm, beside the moisture probe
Reports
°C, daily min / max
03

Rainfall

Rain that actually fell on the garden, set against the record for the area, and how long each fall stays in the soil.

Self-emptying tipping-bucket gauge
Placed
Open ground, clear of canopy drip

0.2 mm

Air and humidity

Heat days, frost nights and dew, measured inside the garden's own microclimate under its canopy.

1.2 m, shaded

Light under the canopy

How much light reaches the understorey through the day and the seasons: a direct read on the canopy closing over.

How it works

From the gate to the record.

We install it

Gardener & Son fit the sign at the gate, the probes in the root zone and the gauge in open ground. Half a day.

It reads the garden

Every five minutes, around the clock. Birds are identified on the sign itself, and only the species name and time are kept.

The record grows

Readings flow into the garden's Ecological Registry record as measured evidence, and into the steward's seasonal update.

What you'll see

Your garden's own season, in numbers.

After rain

How many days each fall keeps the root zone moist, and how that changes as mulch and leaf litter build up year on year.

e.g. 18 mm on Tuesday · soil above 25% for 9 days

Through the seasons

Soil warming in spring, the first frost night, the hottest stretch of summer, all measured where the plants are growing.

e.g. soil at 15 cm passed 15°C on 24 October

Who's around

The species heard each day and over a season, when migrants arrive, and how the chorus changes as the garden matures.

e.g. 14 species this week · first Sacred Kingfisher 2 Nov

Built honest

Measured, recorded, never inflated.

  • The big number is the garden's Registry score. It changes only when the record is reassessed, never automatically from a sensor.
  • Sensor data is added to the record as evidence, labelled as measured at the garden.
  • The sign shows the record and a few live readings. The full data stays with the steward, and the garden's precise location is never published.
  • No camera. The microphone keeps species detections only; no sound is recorded or stored.
  • The display dims at night for neighbours and wildlife.

Questions

Good to know.

Does my garden need to be on the Registry?

Yes. Signal shows the garden's Registry record at the gate and adds its readings to that record, so the founding units are going to registered gardens.

Does it record sound?

No. The microphone listens for birdsong and identifies the species on the sign itself. Only the species name, time and confidence are kept. No audio is ever stored or sent.

Can the readings change our score?

Not by themselves. The score changes only when the record is reassessed. Sensor data is added as evidence, clearly labelled as measured at the garden.

What does it need from the house?

A low-voltage power supply and the garden's Wi-Fi. Everything else, including bird identification, runs on the sign.

Is Signal finished?

Not yet. It's a prototype, and the founding pilot this spring is how we'll finish it. Specifications may change as the pilot runs.
