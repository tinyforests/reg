The proposition · Rec. 02
02The proposition

The Registry recognises private gardens as ecological assets.

Habitat, canopy, soil health, water absorption and connectivity are all being created in backyards across Boroondara. That value is rarely measured, and almost never carried forward. The Registry creates a lasting record of it.

The gap · Rec. 03
03What is missing

Most of the city's private land is doing ecological work off the record.

  • A large proportion of urban land is privately owned — council cannot reach biodiversity and canopy goals through public land alone.
  • Many residents are already improving habitat, soil, water and planting structure.
  • That contribution is mostly invisible in existing council reporting.
  • Valuable ecological knowledge is lost when a house is sold.
  • Gardens are judged aesthetically far more often than by ecological function.

Council already invests in education, plants, grants and biodiversity programs. The Registry helps preserve the evidence of what those efforts create.

Method · Rec. 04
04What each garden record holds

Five areas, assessed at the property level.

01

Biodiversity structure

Ground layer, shrubs, understorey, canopy, plant diversity, indigenous dominance.

02

Soil & water

Soil protection, organic matter, infiltration, passive irrigation, retention, reduced runoff.

03

Habitat complexity

Logs, rocks, hollows, leaf litter, water, nesting opportunities, shelter.

04

Connectivity

Proximity to parks, remnant vegetation, waterways, street trees, nearby habitat gardens.

05

Evidence & stewardship

Photographs, field notes, plant records, observations, change over time, verification.

Boundaries · Rec. 08
08What the Registry is not

Not a plant finder

Plant-selection tools help people decide what to plant. The Registry records what was actually created.

Not citizen science

Species observations tell us what has visited a garden. We record the ecological asset that is there to support them.

Not a certification scheme

No regulatory claims and no certifying authority. A transparent, evidence-based record.

Not a council database

It can complement council programs without requiring council to own or maintain the platform.

Not an address directory

Precise residential addresses are not published. Public profiles are deliberately privacy-conscious.

Not carbon accounting

Focused on biodiversity, habitat, soil, water and connectivity — not credits or offsets.

Privacy · Rec. 09
09Privacy position

Recognition without unnecessary exposure.

  • Public profiles do not reveal a precise residential address — locations generalise to suburb, neighbourhood or a masked map area.
  • Owners control which evidence is displayed publicly.
  • Self-reported and independently verified records are clearly distinguished, and neither is treated as invalid.
  • Participation is voluntary, and consent governs household information.
Permanence · Rec. 10
10The permanence argument

A garden should not lose its memory when ownership changes.

Ecological work can take decades to mature, but the record of it is scattered across invoices, planting plans, photographs and individual recollection. We sometimes describe the Registry as a land title for ecological value — not a legal title, but a persistent record that survives a change of owner.

Network · Rec. 11
11The missing middle

One garden is small. A cluster near a reserve, creek or street-tree corridor becomes ecological infrastructure.

Public reserves are vital, but the land between them matters too. The Registry makes those stepping stones visible — moving from individual good intentions to a recorded habitat network.

Council value · Rec. 12
12Why this may be useful to Boroondara

A biodiversity strategy describes the network at a municipal scale. The Registry helps make the individual properties within it visible.

See where habitat already exists

Identify clusters of habitat gardens and where private land strengthens existing corridors.

Target support where it counts

Direct education, plants and incentives toward the gaps that aggregated records reveal.

Keep the evidence

Retain records of outcomes beyond the life of a single grant, giveaway or program.

Fit · Rec. 13
13How it sits with existing work

Council programs may initiate the change. The Registry helps remember it.

Designed to complement indigenous plant giveaways, Gardens for Wildlife, the Backyard Biodiversity Project, canopy strategies and citizen-science initiatives. We are not duplicating council expertise or asking council to replace anything — we are creating a record that can sit underneath many different programs and preserve their outcomes.

Pilot · Rec. 15
15A sensible pilot

10–20 voluntary gardens, in one neighbourhood or beside an existing corridor.

01

Select willing residents — existing biodiversity-program participants are the natural starting point.

02

Establish a baseline for each garden across the five areas.

03

Protect exact residential locations throughout.

04

Revisit the gardens after an agreed period.

05

Report in aggregate — strengths, gaps and opportunities, no household exposed.

Possible outputs: habitat features recorded · indigenous plant structure · canopy and understorey gaps · water-sensitive features · proximity to public habitat · before-and-after records

Appendix · Rec. 18
AQuestions we expect — method and data
Who verifies the information?
Each record carries its verification level. Some are self-reported; others are reviewed through photographs, documentation or an on-site assessment. We do not hide that distinction, and provisional records are quarantined from verified reporting.
Is the score scientifically validated?
It is a structured ecological assessment framework, not a substitute for formal ecological surveying. We want to work with ecologists, councils and practitioners to improve the method over time.
Could people game the score?
The score is tied to recorded inputs and supporting evidence. Verification levels stay visible, and unsupported claims can be quarantined.
Who owns the data?
Household information remains governed by clear consent and privacy rules. Public-facing information is separated from internal assessment data.
Appendix · Rec. 19
BQuestions we expect — role and reach
Is this a regulatory tool?
No. It is voluntary and recognition-based.
Does council have to maintain it?
No. The Registry operates independently and can provide agreed, aggregated information back to council.
What happens when someone sells?
The ecological record stays attached to the garden profile, while personal ownership information changes or is removed.
Does it favour wealthy households?
An important risk, and one the method is built to resist: it recognises ecological function, not garden expense. Logs, leaf litter, indigenous groundcovers, water retention and reduced chemical use are highly valuable and cost very little.
What about renters and apartments?
The framework can extend to renters, shared gardens, body-corporate landscapes and nature strips. The first version establishes a reliable property-level method.
Will precise property locations be visible?
No. Public profiles mask the address and display only a general area.
